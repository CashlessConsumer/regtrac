# Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India

The policyholder's regulator — in theory. IRDAI licenses insurers and intermediaries, approves products, and sits on top of grievance machinery (Bima Bharosa, insurance ombudsmen) that handles hundreds of thousands of complaints a year, dominated by mis-selling and claim rejections.

## Statute and setup

- **IRDA Act, 1999** ended LIC/GIC's monopoly and created the regulator; constituted 2000. Headquarters: Hyderabad.
- Board: Chairperson + Whole-Time Members, appointed by the Centre.

## What it regulates

- Life, general and health insurers (state-owned and private, 60+)
- Intermediaries: agents, brokers, web aggregators, TPAs, insurance marketing firms
- Reinsurance arrangements, including the GIFT City reinsurance pathway (with IFSCA)

## Powers that matter

- Product approval — now shifting to a "use and file" regime for many categories, which speeds launch and moves consumer risk from pre-approval to post-hoc enforcement
- Licensing and penalties for mis-selling and claim-delay violations
- Policyholder protection regulations: disclosure, claim timelines, health claim charters (2024, with the General Insurance Council)

## Leadership

*(as of 12 Sep 2026 — Chairperson: Ajay Seth since 24 Jul 2025, succeeding Debasish Panda; Whole-Time Member for Life: Swaminathan S Iyer (2025).)*

## For the person paying

- **Grievance route:** Bima Bharosa portal (IRDAI) escalates insurer complaints; the Insurance Ombudsman (cioins.gov.in, 15 offices) awards compensation free of cost. Claim rejection or delay: ombudsman first, court later.
- Mis-selling of ULIPs and traditional plans by bank "relationship managers" is the classic case — the commission structure rewards it; ask for the benefit illustration and surrender value in writing before signing.
- A claim must be repudiated or paid within statutory timelines; delay itself is compensable.

## Watchpoints (capture and accountability)

- The 2023-25 reform wave ("Insurance for All by 2047", use-and-file, FDI liberalisation announced in the 2025 Budget and legislated that year) loosens pre-market checks; consumer protection has to be won in enforcement instead.
- Industry bodies (GI Council, Life Council, IBAI) co-write charters and standard terms — whose draft wins is not published.
- Insurer-side data: grievance volumes by insurer are published; enforcement actions against insurers for repeat grievance patterns are not — the asymmetry that lets bad claims behaviour persist.
