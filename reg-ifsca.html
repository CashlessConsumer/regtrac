India's youngest and most unusual regulator: one authority, all sectors, one square kilometre. Since 1 October 2020, IFSCA has replaced RBI, SEBI, IRDAI and PFRDA inside GIFT City — banking, markets, insurance, pensions, fintech — with a mandate to compete with Singapore and Dubai. Speed is the product; the governance question is whether speed is being regulated.

Statute and setup

  • IFSCA Act, 2019 (enacted Dec 2019); operational 1 Oct 2020.
  • Chairperson + members appointed by the Centre. Chairperson K. Rajaraman's tenure was extended to Oct 2028 in Aug 2026.

What it regulates

  • IFSC Banking Units (56+ banks), capital-market intermediaries, insurance and reinsurance entities (GIFT Re), aircraft and ship leasing, fintech sandbox participants
  • Offshore-rupee products, GIFT-listed securities, diaspora retail products as they open up
  • Within the IFSC, IFSCA rules displace the sectoral regulators' rulebooks entirely

Powers that matter

  • Unified single-window regulation — every licence, every rule, one body
  • Regulatory sandbox with forbearance powers (relaxations from Indian financial rules inside the zone)
  • Cross-regulator negotiation: IFSCA asks SEBI/MoF for enabling changes when its products collide with the mainland perimeter — e.g., the May 2026 SEBI consultation to let online bond platforms distribute IFSC-regulated overseas debt

Leadership

(as of 12 Sep 2026 — Chairperson: K. Rajaraman since Sep 2023, tenure extended till Oct 2028 (Aug 2026); predecessor: Injeti Srinivas, the founding chair.)

For the person paying

  • Mostly business-to-business today, but the pipeline — GIFT-domiciled "Global Indian" accounts, retail diaspora investment products — is aimed at individual savers. When IFSC retail products reach you, your grievance route will be IFSCA's, not RBI's or SEBI's.
  • Watch deposit insurance: IFSC products sit outside DICGC's domestic cover. "Guaranteed" in an IFSC brochure has a different legal meaning.

Watchpoints (capture and accountability)

  • One regulator writing banking + markets + insurance rules at speed, with sandbox forbearance and limited parliamentary scrutiny of IFSC rulebooks — the thinnest accountability-to-power ratio in Indian financial regulation.
  • Boundary bargaining with mainland regulators (bond platforms, reinsurance) happens in consultation papers that read technical but reallocate entire product categories.
  • Leadership extension (2026) with no published selection benchmark — consistent with the ACC-appointment pattern across all regulators.
