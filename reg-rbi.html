The oldest and most powerful financial regulator in India — and the one that touches the most people. The RBI licenses and supervises banks, NBFCs and payment systems, sets interest-rate policy through the Monetary Policy Committee, and runs the ombudsman scheme that is most Indians' only practical route to compensation when a bank or wallet loses their money.

Statute and setup

  • RBI Act, 1934 — the bank began operations on 1 April 1935; nationalised in 1949.
  • Banking Regulation Act, 1949 gives it licensing, supervision and penalty powers over banks.
  • Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 gives it authority over every payment system in India — UPI, cards, wallets, RTGS, NEFT.
  • Central Board: Governor + up to 4 Deputy Governors + government-nominated and regional directors. The Governor is appointed by the Centre (Appointments Committee of the Cabinet) — there is no independent selection process.

What it regulates

  • Scheduled commercial banks, small finance banks, payments banks, RRBs, cooperative banks (banking side)
  • NBFCs, housing finance companies (since Aug 2019, taken from NHB)
  • Payment system operators and intermediaries — including NPCI (UPI, RuPay, AePS), wallet issuers, payment aggregators
  • Credit information companies; asset reconstruction companies; money lenders through state partnership
  • Exchange rate and forex (FEMA, 1999, jointly with the Centre)

Powers that matter

  • Issue, suspend and cancel licences; impose moratoria (the Yes Bank moratorium, 2020, is the template)
  • Monetary policy: statutory inflation target of 4% (+/- 2%) under the 2016 amendments; MPC rate decisions
  • Directions and master directions — the real rulebook; everything from UPI transaction limits to NBFC conduct
  • Recognition of self-regulatory organisations: the SRO-FT framework (draft Jan 2024) produced FACE (Aug 2024) and UFF (Sep 2026) — tracked in SROTrac
  • Depositor protection lever: ₹5 lakh deposit insurance via DICGC (a wholly owned subsidiary)

Leadership

(as of 12 Sep 2026 — see leadership table; Governor: Sanjay Malhotra since Dec 2024; Deputy Governors appointed by the Centre: Poonam Gupta (Apr 2025), Shirish Chandra Murmu (Oct 2025).)

For the person paying

  • Grievance route: complain to your bank/wallet first; if no reply in 30 days or you are unsatisfied, escalate free to the RBI Integrated Ombudsman — cms.rbi.org.in.
  • Zero-MDR on UPI and debit cards is a statutory mandate (from Jan 2020) — if a merchant refuses UPI or adds a "UPI charge", that is a violation, not a policy.
  • Unclaimed deposits sit in RBI's Depositor Education and Awareness fund — the RBI runs drives to return them; check your family's dormant accounts.

Watchpoints (capture and accountability)

  • The top jobs are central-government appointments; the Board mix is government-nominated. Leadership churn (2024-25) happened entirely through ACC decisions.
  • The SRO framework formally hands industry consensus formation (fees, codes, grievance standards) to industry bodies — SROTrac tracks who those members are.
  • Consultation practice: draft master directions invite comments, but the RBI does not systematically publish who commented or what changed — the LobbyWatch gap.
