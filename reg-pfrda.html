Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority

PFRDA Act, 2013 (statutory w.e.f. 1 Feb 2014; interim body since 2003)

The youngest of the big-four regulators. PFRDA runs the National Pension System and Atal Pension Yojana — the retirement money of government employees, since 2004, and any citizen who opts in. Its core consumer problem: subscribers take all the market risk, while fund managers take management fees on other people's retirement.

Statute and setup

  • Created by executive order in 2003; made statutory by the PFRDA Act, 2013 (w.e.f. 1 Feb 2014).
  • Chairperson + members, appointed by the Centre.

What it regulates

  • Pension fund managers under NPS (public and private schemes) and APY
  • Central recordkeeping agencies (CRAs: Protean, KFin) — where your PRAN actually lives
  • Points of presence (banks etc.) that onboard subscribers; retirement advisers
  • The NPS Trust structure, through which the assets are held

Powers that matter

  • Registration and penalty powers over pension funds, CRAs and PoPs
  • Investment-pattern regulations — how much of your money goes to equity, corporate debt, government paper, AIFs
  • Subscriber-protection regulations (2022): exit norms, service standards, withdrawal timelines
  • Scheme design: the 2025 assured-payout and inflation-indexed NPS products were shaped through expert panels the chairperson convenes

Leadership

(as of 12 Sep 2026 — Chairperson: Sivasubramanian Ramann, assumed charge 20 Jun 2025; former Deputy CAG. In office, PFRDA has pushed mobile/UPI onboarding for informal workers and set up a panel on assured 10-year payout options under NPS.)

For the person paying

  • Grievance route: NPS Ombudsman (PFRDA Redressal of Subscriber Grievance Regulations, 2015) after CRA/PoP escalation; APY subscribers ride on bank grievance machinery too.
  • Fund-manager choice matters less than tier and scheme choice: the government default scheme exists for people who do not want to pick.
  • At exit, annuity purchase is mandatory for a large share of the corpus — annuity pricing is set by insurers regulated by IRDAI, not PFRDA. Your pension regulator does not control your pension payout.

Watchpoints (capture and accountability)

  • Pension funds are subsidiaries of the same fund houses and insurers whose products they "compete" on — fee and default-scheme design are the places to watch.
  • Product design by expert panel (assured payout, inflation-linked NPS) is good process, but panel composition and submissions are not published.
  • Coverage push toward informal workers via UPI-based micro-contributions will make PFRDA a mass-market DPI regulator — consumer protection staffing has not visibly scaled to match.
